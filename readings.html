Reading #1: Susan Sontag’s "On Photography"

What does it mean to you to capture images and create graphics?

“While real people are out there killing themselves or other real people, the photographer stays behind his or her camera, creating a tiny element of another world: the image-world that bids to outlast us all”.

Escapism is a prevalent and undeniable theme across different mediums. Addiction, Sex, Work, food, all components of indulgence. In the context of the identity of the voyeur, photographers mimic the indulgence of gazing. To me photography is the act of capturing, like Sontag mentioned, Idealized worlds. I even consider it cowardice presented as the act of documentation. Physically the act of placing an object that allows to create distance from subjects and events feels like curling shyly around a door frame. I really adore the way Sontag’s touched upon this uncanny existence of capturing an idealized mirror of reality. My camera is a tool that allows me to capture something I find beautiful, interesting, horrific, appealing, desirable, without consequence. When creating my work I ask myself, “If you found a pocket of time that felt so surreal and tangible, what would you find?”. Finding moments such as these feel so fragile and feeble, that the only way to know it was real is to capture it. The shutter cements a single frame of a second of time into a tangible feeling. I spend my time curating worlds in photoshop and lightroom curating an image to what I am delirious to think I actually saw. Residing in a romanticized actuality that does not exist. Yet I indulge in it anyway. I seek out to create idealized worlds and moments, hyperfocusing on the dopamine rushes of beauty and unseen worlds. Photography makes me feel whole even in my own strange form of cowardice. I would rather exist in my own heady romanticized daze than actually engage in the real world. I view indulgence as a double edged sword while being aware of its consequences and positives. To me, creating this art helps aid the chaos all humans are collectively born with. The image-world feels the most immovable and so close to reality that it is almost possible. Art has always existed in this fascinating strange grey-area, as subjectivity rules how we understand it. Capturing moments in that ambiguity gives you the autonomy to not be definitive. Nowadays on the internet we become immediately defined by what we choose to post, comment on, like, or reshare. These acts are all intentional, all hurdling towards an end goal be seen by others. We do not have control over how we are perceived but we do have choice in what we want to capture behind our lens. I believe we are shown so many instances of how cruel the real world is that I think hiding behind the lens could be healing. Photographers are given the choice to intervene and capture aspects of its horrors or find tranquility in its beauty. I’ve chosen to dedicate my life to craft that allows me to shape my life, the worlds, and the events I want to capture. It’s selfish and it’s almost hard to admit how badly I always want to feel like I'm somewhere else. To live in the caught breath of awe. To place my confidence in cowardice. Even if it is warped, as Sontag put it, defense against anxiety.

Reading #2: AI Slop and It's Distortion of Reality by Neserine Malik"

What are the detrimental effects of AI in our day and age and its connection to politics?

The state of AI feels like an insidious infection that plagues modern technology and eats away at our capacity to critically think. Your parents. Your grandparents. That one professor you have or your annoying co-worker who shoves brain-rot AI instagram reels in your face during your break. We’ve all become victims to the unavoidable slew of AI content that is continuously shoved down our throats. In her article, Nesrine Malik discusses the concerning and political implications AI seems to bring as it rapidly develops. In its initial upbringing, AI was promoted as harmless uses of generative image-creation that only dared to replicate the visual media it had to source from. Now the software system has continued to output content that can only be described as, like Malik puts it, “AI Slop”. This term is the most accurate description of the meaningless and outright indecent forms of content that are produced by artificial intelligence. The content we see is being produced in such a rapid state that it is almost impossible to monitor and counter. Once these AI worlds of objective take the reins of algorithms, it gets more and more difficult to curate a feed that reflects reality. Within her piece, Malik makes a brilliant connection to how facism and AI has become intertwined, now affecting the US politically.. Change is inspired by diversity and variation in thought that inspires critical thinking and inclusion of varied perspectives. The human mind can understand complex patterns of society but AI can not. Like Malik mentions, AI recognizes patterns, patterns that are based on pre-existing data. Data that cannot factor in the nuance of social ethics, inherently upholding the notions of conservatism. It’s honestly quite jarring to witness how conservatives are weaponizing AI. Conservatism thrives on its lack of empathetic ideals, making AI a perfect medium for the cold nature of the ideology. To me, the surge of AI seems to be turning the world into a direction that might be very difficult to turn back from. With its use being encouraged by employers, tech professionals and even some artists, it seems to be a form of complacency that has fallen over the general population. Its existence has become an embedded parasite into our technologies, at first infecting our screens but is now slowly suffocating the world.

Reading #3: Heela: The schoolgirl ‘My stolen future’

What lies in the strength of a mulit-medium story is the masterful use of contrast. I chose the heartbreaking story of a young woman named Heela, whose future was cruelly ripped away from her. I think illustration as a medium can be used as a very creative and meaningful way to convey a story. In this context I also think it is the most respectful. I believe that if her circumstances were pictured in a manner that was live action, I would’ve found it very distasteful. When I think of the ethics of a live re-creation I think of Ryan Murphy. His recent work concerning mass murderers and serial killers has come to quite a lot of controversy. This is due to the psychology of his re-creations and the questionable motives that seem to be hidden behind his work. When an actor is forced to recreate a depiction, they must force their being and look with the mindset of a “killer”. A killer who has made horrific impacts on both communities and families. This is a very hard thing to ask of and can lead to a skewed version of a killer. Many of his series attempt to bring “empathy” to the killers that ultimately just end up undermining their actions entirely. With the medium of illustration, one artist or a few artists has control over the depiction rather than the many others who can put their own influence into a performance. Another contrast is that an artist can alter depiction in several creative ways. There are so many limits to live action video. In this story I think it was quite appropriate to use still illustrations. You can reflect that focus on impact and depictions that are not limited by reality. The use of text also provides background information to the illustration, photos, and videos pictured in the story. Though an image can convey an emotion or a story, the combination of images with text can directly let us know the story itself. The text is literal and intentional, leaving itself no room to be mis-interpreted like an illustration or an image. Contrasted to the photographs, I think the illustrations give a better telling of the emotions of the story. LIke text, photographs have the ability to be used as evidence. In this story I don’t think manipulating an image would serve the story for its author and for the audience. Again, illustration brings infinite possibility and reveals what the author is comfortable with showing. For example. If we had a photo of Heelas rapist, yet she wasn’t comfortable showing his identity, then what? The photo could be blurred or manipulated, yet it takes away the accountability that I think Heela wanted to show. The use of illustration would be considered the next best thing.A man with a blurred face is a man with a blurred face. Even if illustrated, a man with features is real and there is no room for speculation. Illustration is also a very masterful way of presenting the past. The use of a photo for Heela’s present brings us directly to her life after her tragic past. When I reflect, I think about how beautiful it is to see her outside of her story. Her story becomes less idealized and the photographs further cement the validity of her testament.
